# UX Principles

## 1. Start with the user's job

The first screen of each product surface must expose the most likely next action. Avoid landing-page explanations inside operational tools.

## 2. Progressive commitment

Ask only for information needed at the current step. Preserve booking progress through authentication. Place sign-in at the latest safe point before account or payment work.

## 3. One clear decision

Each step has one primary action. Secondary actions remain available without competing visually. Replace long native-looking option dumps with purposeful controls such as segmented choices, menus, calendars, steppers, and search.

## 4. Backend truth

The interface presents verified state; it does not infer payment, membership, assignment, referral, credit, or payout success. Pending and unavailable states must be explicit.

## 5. Privacy by default

Show the minimum data needed for the current role and moment. Address becomes available to an assigned Zooter after acceptance. Access codes and sensitive notes remain behind the separate approved time-bound flow.

## 6. Recover without losing work

Validation errors preserve input. Offline and session-expiry states explain what is saved and what to do next. Retrying must be idempotent where the action is consequential.

## 7. Native where it matters

Respect iOS and Android navigation, back behaviour, keyboard, accessibility, haptics, widgets, and assistant expectations. Shared brand does not require identical platform chrome.

## 8. Motion communicates

Motion confirms selection, continuity, success, interruption, or hierarchy. It must not delay input, conceal state, or become ambient decoration.

## 9. Human escalation remains visible

Concierge and self-service flows must offer safe handoff for payment, safety, legal, privacy, account-risk, or unresolved service matters.

## 10. Design for interruption

Booking, Zooter jobs, support, and account recovery must resume safely after backgrounding, slow networks, duplicated taps, or device changes.
