# Facebook Groups — reply templates

**This is the real Facebook channel.** Not the Page. The Page is where selling is expected; the
groups are where the demand actually is, and they are also the fastest place to get thrown out of.

*"Can anyone recommend someone reliable?"* appears weekly in every Fourways, Bryanston, Sea Point and
Table View community group. That is **demand with intent, in a named suburb** — precisely what
launch gate 5 needs, and it costs nothing but behaving well.

---

## ⛔ The five rules, and they are not style advice

1. **The founder participates as a person, from the personal account.** Never the Page. A Page
   commenting in a community group reads as an advert and is moderated as one.
2. **Answer the question that was asked.** If someone asks about load-shedding, answer about
   load-shedding. Being useful when there is nothing in it for you is the entire mechanism.
3. **Never pitch unprompted.** When someone asks for a recommendation and Xoot genuinely fits: say so
   **once**, **disclose the connection in the same sentence**, and stop. No follow-up, no DM, no
   second comment.
4. **Read the group's rules before the first comment.** Most ban promotion outright; some have a
   designated business-post day. Both are workable. Ignoring either is not.
5. **Never argue.** A founder's public argument in a community group is the screenshot that outlives
   the company.

⚠️ **The disclosure is not optional and not a formality.** Recommending your own company without
saying so is a misleading representation under **CPA s41** *and* the thing that gets you removed
from the group. It costs seven words.

⛔ **Do not post any of these until gate 2 clears.** Every one of them ends somewhere a person can
go, and right now that place is a GoDaddy placeholder. FBG-05 is the exception.

## FBG-01 — Someone asks for a cleaner recommendation

> Hi — I should say upfront that I run Xoot, so I'm not a neutral answer here.
>
> What I'd actually suggest regardless of who you use: ask whoever you're considering how they
> handle it when the person who was booked can't make it. That's the question that separates a
> service from a phone number, and almost nobody asks it until the morning it happens.
>
> Happy to answer anything about how we do it if it's useful.

**Why it works:** it gives a genuinely useful question the reader can take to anyone, and the
disclosure is the first line rather than a footnote.
⛔ No price. No availability claim. No link unless someone asks.

## FBG-02 — Someone had a bad experience with a cleaning service

> That's a rough one, sorry.
>
> Declaring an interest — I run a company in this space, so take it accordingly. The thing that
> usually causes this isn't the person who came, it's that there was no agreement about what
> "finished" meant. Worth writing down the specific rooms and the specific tasks before the next one
> starts, whoever you book. It makes the awkward conversation unnecessary.

⛔ **Never** name or imply the company they had a problem with, and never say "that wouldn't happen
with us." You do not know that, and with zero completed bookings you especially do not know that.
`APPROVE / EDIT / KILL`

## FBG-03 — Someone asks what a fair rate is

> I run a home-services company, so I'll be careful here rather than useless.
>
> I'm not going to quote you a number, partly because it depends heavily on size and frequency and
> partly because we're in the middle of getting our own pricing straight and I'd rather say nothing
> than say something I have to correct.
>
> What I would say: check the hourly rate implied by whatever quote you get against the national
> minimum wage for domestic workers. If it doesn't clear it comfortably once transport is accounted
> for, that's not a bargain, it's a problem waiting to be yours.

⚠️ **This template is the reason the no-price rule is survivable in a group.** Saying "I'd rather say
nothing than say something I have to correct" is more credible than a number, and it is true.
`APPROVE / EDIT / KILL`

## FBG-04 — Someone asks about safety / vetting

> Full disclosure, I run Xoot.
>
> The honest answer for the whole category is that "vetted" means very different things at different
> companies and almost nobody defines it. Worth asking any service: what specifically was checked,
> by whom, and how recently.
>
> I'd rather you asked us that question than took our word for it.

⛔ **Do not describe Xoot's own screening in a group comment.** The homepage claims were reworded
this month for a reason — the previous version described questions asked at sign-up as though they
were completed training. Point at the site once it is reachable; do not re-improvise the claim in a
comment where nobody is reviewing it.
`APPROVE / EDIT / KILL`

## FBG-05 — Someone asks about becoming a cleaner / getting work

> I'm not able to give you a useful answer on this yet, and I'd rather say that than string you
> along — we're not open for applications.
>
> When we are, it'll be posted properly rather than in a comment.

⛔ **This is the only template that is safe to use before gate 2, and it is safe precisely because it
promises nothing.** Anything warmer becomes recruitment, and recruitment is blocked until employment
classification is settled under **LRA s200A** — a person below the BCEA threshold is *presumed* an
employee if any of seven factors applies. A friendly "DM me and I'll let you know" in a group is a
recruitment communication.

## Groups worth joining, and the order

Join and read for **two weeks before commenting once.** A new account that arrives and immediately
answers a recommendation request is transparent, and the moderators have seen it a thousand times.

| Priority | Area | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The launch suburb's own group | Gate 5 is supply *and* demand in **one** suburb. Everything else is a distraction until that suburb works |
| 2 | Adjacent suburbs to the launch suburb | Where suburb two comes from |
| 3 | City-wide "recommendations" groups | Higher volume, much lower intent, stricter moderation |

⛔ **Do not join twenty groups.** Five, read properly, in one metro. The failure mode here is volume,
and it looks exactly like effort.

**Metric:** enquiries by suburb. Not comments posted, not groups joined.
