## The eight patterns worth adopting

### 1. Validated idea × favourite idea

The most useful sentence in the set: *"find a way to connect validated ideas with your favourite
ideas."* Attention comes from a packaging already proven; retention comes from an idea only you
would have had. Craig's worked example: he wanted to write about the nine stages of ego development
(nobody wants that), shipped it as *How to become more intelligent than 99% of people* (500K
views), and the people who arrived got the weird thing.

**Xoot's version:** the validated packaging is the build-in-public founder-post format; the
favourite idea is a real defect in a real payments system in a real emerging market. Nobody else is
publishing that.

### 2. Broad title, narrow body

*"Get people through the door first, then you can explore the house."* Titles broad; the argument
inside as niche as you like. The named failure mode is the esoteric title on a good essay.

### 3. The outline is 80–90% of the work

Craig spends three to four days on an outline and writes nothing until every part is ticked. Then
writing is just adding words. The framework under every outline is **problem → insight → solution**,
and the insight slot is where the favourite idea goes.

### 4. One 1% improvement per week

Open one post you admire. Work out what every sentence is doing at the mechanism level. Extract
**one** principle. Apply it this week. ~80 essays later he describes his growth as a straight line.
This is the cheapest possible improvement loop and it fits the cost discipline in the plan.

### 5. The idea museum, and where unique ideas come from

Save what works to a swipe file. But validated ideas are only half: *"you look where nobody else is
looking"* — books, accounts with no traction, articles passed around in group chats, another
platform entirely. **Cross-platform idea arbitrage is the highest-leverage version.** Studying the
same feed as everyone else produces the same content as everyone else.

### 6. Ideas beat hooks beat formats

Stated flatly: quality of ideas matters more than hooks, and more than the viral formats everyone
optimises for. This contradicts most of what the growth playbooks chase, and it is the more
defensible position for a brand whose proposition is trustworthiness.

### 7. The ten attention mechanisms

Specific numbers · pattern interrupts · negativity bias · calling out a specific target · naming the
problem · readability · confidence without hedging · the benefit to the reader · social proof ·
warning or caution.

> ⚠️ **Two are constrained for Xoot, and the constraint is not optional.**
> "Confidence in absolutes" is described in the source as *deliberately over-stating so people act*.
> Under **CPA s41 a misleading representation is misleading regardless of intent**, and this repo's
> documented failure mode is optimism stated as present fact. And *negativity bias* stops at the
> line where the negative thing is a person: never a competitor, never a Xooter, never a customer.
> **The villain is a system, never a person.**

### 8. AI is a prototype, never an output

Unanimous across all five. *"Do not trust the default output."* Craig's harder version: he deleted
his thinking-partner prompt because it was making him lazy, and now hand-writes every idea before AI
touches the outline. He keeps an **anti-AI-patterns document** he pastes into every session.

Two consequences: the human edit pass on anything published under the founder's name is not
optional, and the durable defence against AI-detection is the same thing that makes writing good —
first-person specificity a model could not invent. That is already §6 of the growth plan; the
transcripts arrive at it independently.

## The funnel mathematics — hold loosely

From Marcos Ruiz, and worth exactly what one unaudited creator claim is worth:

- **Follower-to-call ratio** — roughly 50 followers per booked call in B2B, 100–200 in B2C. An order
  of magnitude, not a number. Xoot is a local marketplace, not a consulting funnel.
- **The 3-second credibility test** — a visitor learns who you are, what you do, and what you can do
  for them within three seconds of landing on the profile.
- **PESTO pillars** — Personal, Expertise, Social proof, Trending, Opinions.
- **Bottleneck analysis** — impressions → profile visits → followers → conversations → bookings.
  Work only on the step with the worst ratio.

**Bottleneck analysis is the same discipline as the weekly loop's "name the binding constraint",
and it is unusable until Phase 1 instrumentation exists**, because every one of those ratios is
currently unmeasured.

## Explicitly rejected

The advice to *"only read Substack posts over 300 likes and only watch YouTube videos over 100K
views"* is a good calibration exercise and a bad information diet — the same video says three
minutes later that unique ideas come from where nobody is looking. Take the calibration, drop the
diet.
