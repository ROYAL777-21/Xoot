## ⚠️ Three things to settle before any of this is produced

### 1. "Multiple avatars" is right for testing and wrong as a commitment

The corpus is unusually direct about this:

> *"I think it's unwise to have two entirely different avatars. And the price of the business that
> you want at a million a month is giving up the very small business… If you're willing to pay that
> price, then it's an easy decision."*
> — [Hormozi, *Helping a Marketing Agency Get to $1M/Month*, 01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rMMOHeBzA50&t=90s)

He also warns, in the same segment, that a cheaply-acquired segment can be the worst one you have,
and that **you cannot tell until you have LTV, not just CAC** — so do not restructure on two weeks
of data.

**How that resolves here.** Xoot has **zero completed bookings**. There is no avatar to be loyal to
yet, and running one avatar on a guess would be the more expensive mistake. So:

- **Now (discovery):** run all four avatars below, deliberately, to find out which is real.
- **After ~20 bookings:** rank by who actually books, rebooks and complains least — then **cut to
  one primary and one secondary.** Write the cut into the growth log with what it cost.
- **Never:** maintain four avatars as a permanent strategy. That is the state he is warning about.

### 2. The AI presenter cannot be a customer, ever

The founder asked for an AI influencer. **The workable version is a branded presenter; the version
that ends in a complaint is a fabricated customer.**

Under **CPA s41** a misleading representation is misleading regardless of intent, and the
Advertising Regulatory Board's code treats testimonials as requiring a real, identifiable person.
`BRAND_GUIDE` independently bans *"fake reviews, invented credentials"*.

> **⚠️ UPDATED 2026-09-02 by founder decision.** She may now speak in the first person — *"I love
> how fast this books"* — and **in exchange the render style changed from photoreal to stylised
> illustration**. The two are locked together: a cartoon character enthusing about a product is
> advertising; a photorealistic person describing a clean that never happened is a testimonial for a
> service with zero completed bookings. Full reasoning in `ai-presenter-brief.md`.

| The AI presenter may | The AI presenter may never |
|---|---|
| Present, host, narrate, demonstrate | Be rendered photorealistically or be mistakable for a photograph |
| **Speak in first person as a brand character** | Be presented as a real, specific customer |
| Express warmth and enthusiasm about Xoot | Give a star rating, a numeric review, or a before/after account |
| Deliver factual lines about how booking works | Appear as a Xooter or a member of staff |
| Be consistent and recognisable across assets | Describe an outcome the product cannot deliver today |

**Every asset carries an AI-generated marker.** Meta and TikTok both require AI-content disclosure,
and a brand caught undisclosed loses more than the asset was worth. This is not caution for its own
sake — the account exists to earn trust from women deciding whether to let a stranger into their
home.

### 3. None of this is posted until the domain works

`xoot.co.za` currently serves a GoDaddy placeholder. Driving attention to it is the specific mistake
`growth-levers-2026.md` finding 5 quantifies: bad word of mouth means **reaching three times as many
people** to get the same customer. Produce the assets now, post them after DNS.

## The lifestyle positioning — young professionals

`locked-commercial-facts.md` sets the position as **premium managed home-care infrastructure** and
forbids "cheap cleaning" or "loose gig marketplace". A lifestyle brand for young professionals is
compatible with that, and it is a sharpening rather than a change.

**The insight the category keeps missing:** competitors sell *a clean house*. That is a chore
outcome. Young professionals in Cape Town and Johannesburg are not buying a clean house — they are
buying **the Sunday back**, and the end of the low-grade guilt of walking past the same pile for a
week.

**Brand line for this segment:** *Your home. Handled.* — already the locked promise, and it happens
to be precisely right for this avatar. Do not invent a second one.

**Register:** warm, calm, practical, quietly aspirational. Never `cheap / budget / discount /
bargain`. Never fake urgency. Sell **relief and time**, not labour and not price.

## The four avatars

Each has a name for shorthand, a real objection, and a different reason to buy. **The objection is
the most important column** — it is what the hook has to defeat.

| # | Avatar | Who | What they are actually buying | The objection that stops them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The Reclaimed Weekend** | 26–34, professional, rents or owns a flat, no kids, long hours | Their Saturday back | *"I can do it myself, I just don't."* Guilt, not money |
| **B** | **The Host** | 28–40, Airbnb or frequent-guest home | A turnover that never slips | *"A stranger, unsupervised, between guests."* Trust and timing |
| **C** | **The Household Manager** | 32–45, kids, dual income, time-poor | One less thing that is hers by default | *"Briefing someone new costs more time than doing it."* Continuity |
| **D** | **The Returner** | Any age, has had a bad cleaner or an unreliable one | Reliability, provable | *"I've been let down before."* Proof, not promises |

**Avatar A is the lifestyle segment the founder named**, and it is the right one to lead on: highest
social-media density, most likely to share, cheapest to reach organically. **Avatar D converts best
and is the smallest** — it needs gate 6's real reviews to exist at all.

⚠️ **Avatar B is a different business in disguise.** Airbnb turnover is B2B-ish, has different
timing pressure, different price tolerance and different failure costs. It is the segment most
likely to become the "two entirely different avatars" problem. Test it, but flag it: if it wins,
that is a strategic decision, not a campaign result.

## The hooks — five families, mapped to awareness stage

Hormozi's five stages of awareness (unaware → most aware) decide which hook can work. Broad
audiences need curiosity; ready buyers need directness. **The same hook cannot serve both, and
measuring them together is how a test tells you nothing.**

| # | Hook family | Awareness stage | Shape | Works for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **The guilt name** | Unaware | Name the feeling before naming the service | A, C |
| **H2** | **The time arithmetic** | Problem-aware | Make the hidden cost countable | A, C |
| **H3** | **The trust mechanic** | Solution-aware | Show the actual vetting step, not the claim | B, D |
| **H4** | **The continuity promise** | Solution-aware | Same person, remembered preferences | B, C |
| **H5** | **The direct offer** | Most aware | Price, scope, book | All, but only at the end |

### Written hooks, ready to shoot

**H1 — the guilt name** *(A, C · unaware)*
- "You've walked past that same pile for eleven days. It's not laziness. It's Tuesday, again."
- "The dishes aren't the problem. The five minutes of dread before the dishes is the problem."
- "Nobody's home is as clean as their Instagram. Ours isn't either. That's the whole business."

**H2 — the time arithmetic** *(A, C · problem-aware)*
- "Four hours, every Saturday. That's twenty-six days a year. What would you do with twenty-six days?"
- "You earn more per hour than a clean costs per hour. You are losing money to do it yourself."
- "The clean takes four hours. The thinking about the clean takes all week."

**H3 — the trust mechanic** *(B, D · solution-aware)*
- "No Xooter is activated until a person has reviewed their ID, their address, their bank details and two references. A person. Not an algorithm."
- "Every profile shows a reliability score, a completion rate and a repeat-client count. You see the record before you book."
- "Here's what we check before someone gets your address." *(show the actual admin review fields)*

**H4 — the continuity promise** *(B, C · solution-aware)*
- "Book Nomsa again. She already knows about the cat, the good glasses, and the door that sticks."
- "The second clean is better than the first, because we remembered."
- "Your Xooter, not a Xooter."

**H5 — the direct offer** *(all · most aware)*
- "Four hours. R250. Booked in ninety seconds." ⚠️ *Was R280 — a price the server never charged.
  Corrected 2026-09-04 to the ZD-037 ladder. This is a hook a renderer typesets verbatim, so the
  figure and the four hours travel together or neither is used.*
- "Your home. Handled. [Suburbs]."

⚠️ **H3 and H4 both make claims the product must actually keep.** The vetting line is true today —
it is the reworded homepage claim from `NEEDS-FOUNDER` §4. **"Book Nomsa again" needs favourite-Xooter
continuity to actually work.** Verify before shooting, or the ad writes a cheque gate 3 has to cash.

## The form factors

**TikTok is primary, Instagram secondary, sequential.** 29.1M South African TikTok users 18+ against
Instagram's 8.6M (`company-social-playbook.md`), and Ralston's Eye of Sauron rule: a solo operator
running four platforms produces diluted work on all four.

| # | Form factor | Length | Where | Best hooks | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **AI presenter piece to camera** | 12–20s | TikTok, Reels | H1, H2 | The founder's requested format. Cheapest to iterate — the script changes, the presenter does not |
| **F2** | **Screen recording of the real booking flow** | 15–30s | TikTok, Reels | H4, H5 | The single most credible asset available, and it costs nothing but a screen recorder |
| **F3** | **Text-on-still, no face** | static | Instagram, X | H1, H2 | Ben Meer's 12-second Reel finding: the payload in the first line. Highest output per hour |
| **F4** | **Before / after, real homes only** | 8–15s | TikTok, Reels | H2 | ⚠️ **Requires a real completed booking and written consent.** Blocked until gate 6 |
| **F5** | **Founder to camera** | 30–60s | X, LinkedIn | H3 | Founder account, different register — see `founder-voice.md`. Never reuse company copy here |
| **F6** | **The trust walkthrough** | 30–45s | TikTok, YouTube | H3 | Screen-record the admin vetting fields. Shows the mechanism instead of asserting it |

**F2 is underrated and should be first.** It is real, it cannot be faked, it needs no budget, and it
is the closest thing available to Ralston's "proof over promise" before real bookings exist.

## The test grid — week one

**Formats and topics are tested independently.** A winning format transfers across topics; a winning
topic does not transfer across formats. Post one idea in one format and a good result tells you
nothing about which caused it.

| Cell | Avatar | Hook | Form | What it is testing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | H1 | F1 | Does the AI presenter hold attention at all? |
| 2 | A | H1 | F3 | Same hook, no presenter — is the presenter adding or costing? |
| 3 | A | H2 | F1 | Same format, different hook family — is it the hook or the face? |
| 4 | C | H4 | F2 | Does the real product out-perform produced creative? |
| 5 | D | H3 | F6 | Does showing the mechanism beat claiming it? |
| 6 | B | H4 | F2 | Is the Host avatar real, or a distraction? |

**Six cells, two variables moved at a time, no more.** Cells 1 and 2 are the only pair that isolates
the AI presenter — that comparison is the point of the week, because it decides whether to keep
paying for it.

**Measure at 72 hours:** impressions, profile visits, replies, saves. **Rank on profile visits per
1,000 impressions, never reach.** On every account measured, the highest-reach post had the worst
engagement rate.

**No pre-registered thresholds.** `@TheRealSkosana` has 1 follower and the company accounts have no
baseline; a threshold without a baseline is an invented number graded against itself. **Week one
measures. Week two tests.**

## The AI presenter — production spec

Higgsfield, and the reusable-identity route rather than one-off generations, because consistency
across dozens of assets is the entire value.

**Route:** train a **Soul** (reusable character), then generate with `soul_2` + `soul_id`. One-off
`soul_cast` generations drift between assets, which defeats the purpose.

**Character brief:**
- South African, late 20s, warm rather than glossy, reads as a neighbour rather than a model
- Wardrobe and palette from the design kit, so she is recognisable at thumbnail size
- Environments: a real-feeling Cape Town or Joburg flat — lived in, not a showroom. The category's
  visual cliché is an empty white kitchen, and it is why every cleaning ad looks the same
- **Visibly stylised.** Not attempting photorealism, precisely so it is never mistaken for a
  photograph of a real person

**She is a presenter. Give her a name that reads as a brand character, never a testimonial voice.**

⚠️ **Generating costs credits.** Preflight with `get_cost` before any batch, and the Soul training
run (5–20 reference images) is the one deliberate up-front spend. **Not started — awaiting the
founder's go-ahead on the character brief**, because training the wrong character and discovering it
across thirty assets is the expensive failure mode.
