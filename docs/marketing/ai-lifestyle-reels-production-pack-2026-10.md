## Governing creative direction

**Character:** Use Persona 01 from ai-presenter-brief.md, consistently recognisable, unmistakably **stylised editorial illustration**, not photoreal. The first-person persona is a fictional brand narrator, never a real customer, Xooter or staff member. Original dialogue voiced in natural South African English; conversational rhythm, imperfect micro-pauses, small digressions, no unnatural American pitchman cadence. Never mimic a named living creator's voice.

**World:** Ordinary Johannesburg apartment, occupied rather than aspirational showroom. Unmatched mug, half-read magazine, yesterday's tote bag, towel on chair, dishes, groceries. No stigmatising mess. Warm sun, room tone, domestic sounds. Illustrations should feel observational, not glossy "cleaning company" ads. Distinct Xoot colours/wordmark used sparingly and only if brand kit verified.

**Truth:** The series contains **fictional lifestyle scenarios**, not depictions of actual Xoot bookings. Avoid "my cleaner arrived", "I booked Xoot", "they changed my life", worker interviews, receipt screenshots, ratings, before/after results, citywide availability, guaranteed arrival times, unapproved offers or prices. Mention Xoot only as a concept, or show the real verified product UI when cleared. Before publishing, verify domain, booking readiness, area, current brand mark and product claims against live operational facts. No direct booking CTA while payment/domain/service gates are unresolved.

**Disclosure:** Burn in "AI-generated Xoot character • fictional story" small but legible for every frame and use Instagram's AI-content disclosure setting where applicable. The handle/bio must identify her as the Xoot brand character, not a separate real person. The viewer should understand the situation without looking for fine print.

**Structure:** Each has a human problem in the first 1–2 seconds, one idea, visually varied but not frantic cut points, and a conversational finish. At most one Xoot name drop if a creative beat earns it; no coupon, no fake friend recommendation, no forced "link in bio". Distinguish the soft organic storytelling from clearly branded paid ads and use appropriate branded-content disclosure where required.

**Production spec:** 9:16 safe crop, ideally 1080×1920, original licensed audio/music, clean synced closed captions, speech peaking comfortably under clipping, avoid captions across lower UI bar. Make a 5-second hook variant for each format to test separately, not by simultaneously changing multiple variables. All scripts are flexible guides — voice actors should say them like thoughts, not recital.

## REEL 01 — "Sunday became a second job" (domestic micro-vlog)

**Audience:** time-poor Johannesburg professionals. **Format:** original slice-of-life vlog, Grace-inspired only at the level of genre. **Target:** 70–85 s. **Hypothesis:** observational narrative generates sustained viewing and local identification without a sales pitch. **Cover:** "Why is Sunday my busiest day?"

### Spoken script / beat-by-beat directions

| Time | Picture / camera / edit | Spoken audio |
|---|---|---|
| 0–03 | Open mid-action. Illustrated camera slightly off-level; her hand holds a grocery bag and keys while a single sock lies in doorway. Not an establishing logo shot. | "Why does Sunday feel like a second job?" |
| 03–12 | Kettle switches on; she rubs sleepy eyes; sunlight on dishes. J-cut the next sentence over brewing tea. | "Because I woke up and made a list. Laundry. Groceries. Change the sheets. Everything I pretended I didn't see all week." |
| 12–24 | Medium shot from kitchen counter; old mug, fruit, tote. Hand-held feel, but stable enough to read her expression. | "And I always say I'll do it quickly. Then it's twelve-thirty, I've done one thing, and I'm annoyed at a basket of clothes. As if the basket did something to me." |
| 24–39 | Quiet montage: turn on music, open window, fold one t-shirt, pull a chair out of walkway. Leave some unfilled room sound. | "I used to think being on top of life meant doing every single thing myself. Like, why? Who made that a rule?" |
| 39–54 | Sit on counter stool eating toast. Eyes away from camera, return briefly. | "I can enjoy looking after my place *and* admit that I don't want it to swallow my whole weekend. Those two things can exist together." |
| 54–68 | Close-up phone with notifications, not Xoot app unless real approved recording; put it face down. Quiet afternoon light, she opens a book. | "I'm actually trying something new. One thing off the list, then I go outside. The world doesn't end if the rest waits until tomorrow." |
| 68–80 | She laughs slightly at a second sock found under sofa; cut on laugh, not a big branded end card. Small corner Xoot mark after 74s. | "Although if someone can explain why socks travel alone in this flat, please do." |

**Directing notes:** Avoid incessant selfie eye contact. Use visual breathing space and real foley (kettle, bag, page flip). Acknowledge the character as synthetic visually. **Xoot presence:** small corner mark/end caption, not verbal pitch. One subtle caption line: "There's more to a weekend than getting everything done. Your home. Handled. — Xoot". **Comment invitation:** "What's the one chore you keep putting off?"

**Alternative hook A/B:** A "Why does Sunday feel like a second job?" / B "It's 12:30 and I've only changed the sheets." Test the opening alone with all other frames identical.

## REEL 02 — "Can we talk while I chop?" (food-prep storytime)

**Audience:** conversational creator viewers and overextended household organisers. **Format:** countertop fixed-camera storytime, rhythmic vegetable chopping and real pauses. **Target:** 55–70 s. **Hypothesis:** mundane parallel action helps viewers stay while the spoken point develops; must validate in our account. **Cover:** "Something I realised while cooking".

### Spoken script / beats

| Time | Picture / camera / edit | Spoken audio |
|---|---|---|
| 0–04 | Close microphone to knife-on-board sound. Medium side angle, illustrated sliced peppers, onion, bowl. Cut while she is already speaking. | "Okay, can I ask you something while I cut these peppers?" |
| 04–13 | Chop, glance at camera once, return to board. | "Why is it that whenever someone says, 'What are you doing this weekend?' the answer is always, 'First I need to sort out the house'?" |
| 13–26 | Scrape peppers into a bowl; real 1-second chopping-only beat. | "Like, I get it. You want your space to feel nice. So do I. But it's so weird how the whole weekend becomes one long list of things you haven't done." |
| 26–39 | Switch to tomatoes; small quick smile at own observation, no broad acting. | "And the thing is, no one's actually grading us. There's no person arriving on Monday to check whether you folded the towels properly." |
| 39–52 | Wipe knife; shift shoulders; slightly slower speech. | "I've started thinking: what actually needs *my* attention, and what just needs to get done? Not the same question." |
| 52–63 | Toss vegetables into bowl. Let knife and water sounds lead. | "Anyway. That's my little thought for today. Also... why did I cut enough onion for eight people?" |

**Xoot presence:** The Reel is posted by Xoot; Xoot is named once in the *caption*, not in the story. "For the list that never seems to finish. Xoot — Your home. Handled. A fictional AI character, not a customer account." If the offer isn't ready, do **not** append "book now". **Question:** "What's permanently at the bottom of your list?"

**Directing notes:** Hands/knife geometry must be correct frame by frame, no fingers through food, no disappearing utensils. Slow head movement; voice should be conversational, sometimes quiet. If generated video makes chopping wrong, use close-up illustrated still animation and truthful foley, or use licensed actual hands-only food footage carefully labelled as mixed media.

## REEL 03 — "The Saturday group chat" (POV comedy skit)

**Audience:** young working adults, friends sharing relatable situations. **Format:** illustrated two-personality split-screen/quick costume-free cuts, not a testimonial. **Target:** 28–40 s. **Hypothesis:** social identity humour prompts friend-to-friend sends. **Cover:** "POV: It's finally Saturday".

| Time | Picture / camera / edit | Script |
|---|---|---|
| 0–03 | Split screen: "Group chat" / "Me, looking at the laundry". Ping sound. | Friend bubble (read by same actress in a different tempo): "Breakfast at eleven?" |
| 03–10 | Hard cut to presenter staring at overflowing-but-normal laundry basket. | "Eleven? Friend. The washing machine and I have a meeting." |
| 10–18 | Insert: friend types "Bring the washing machine." Presenter stops folding. | "Don't tempt me. It hasn't seen outside in years." |
| 18–29 | She puts down towel, grabs jacket; leave basket partially folded. | "Okay, I'll be there at twelve. And I refuse to feel guilty about one unfolded towel." |
| 29–35 | Freeze over door close. Natural light, little sound tag. | On-screen: "Some Saturdays should be Saturdays." |

**Brand:** No spoken pitch. Xoot watermark only. **Comment prompt:** "Tag the friend who schedules things before you've even had coffee." **Safety:** not an actual messaging screenshot; mock chat is clearly fictional. **Variation:** change just the comic button line, not talent/length/visuals.

## REEL 04 — "A reset that doesn't eat your day" (genuinely useful micro-tutorial)

**Audience:** search-led organisation and home-routine viewers. **Format:** three-step instructional micro-lesson with unobtrusive timestamps. **Target:** 32–45 s. **Hypothesis:** viewers will save/share specific repeatable utility more than a generic tidy montage. **Cover:** "When you have 15 minutes".

| Time | Picture / camera / edit | Script |
|---|---|---|
| 0–03 | Timer starts at 15:00; messy-but-believable countertop in illustration. | "If you've got fifteen minutes, don't try to clean your entire home." |
| 03–13 | Return cups to kitchen; only a few items. Text: "1 — Collect". | "First five: collect the stuff that's in the wrong room. Don't organise. Just return it." |
| 13–24 | Wipe one main surface. Text: "2 — One surface". | "Next five: choose the one surface you'll actually see all evening." |
| 24–34 | Open window, change cushion, wash single mug. Text: "3 — One comfort". | "Last five: one thing that makes the place nicer to be in. Open a window. Fix the sofa. Whatever feels good." |
| 34–42 | Timer ends; importantly home not magically spotless. | "That's it. A reset, not a punishment." |

**Brand:** Xoot small mark, final caption "Small resets count. Your home. Handled." **Measure:** saves per reach and profile visits per 1,000 local impressions. Avoid false claims that this cleans the whole home.

## REEL 05 — "The sounds of finally being home" (sensory/ASMR micro-film)

**Audience:** slow-living and quiet/home aesthetic viewers. **Format:** sound-first, little or no speech, real-felt tactile details. **Target:** 22–35 s. **Hypothesis:** a sensory open and natural loop can raise completion/replay; no promise of reach. **Cover:** "That first quiet moment at home".

| Time | Picture / camera / edit | Sound / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–04 | Keys land on a small ceramic tray. Close clean foley. | Text: "After a very long day…" |
| 04–09 | Shopping bag touches table, fridge hum in distance. | No speech; soft thud and rustle |
| 09–16 | Water fills a glass, balcony curtain moves, kettle clicks. | Quiet water, soft room tone |
| 16–24 | Book opens on a cleared chair; feet tucked beneath presenter, no face close-up. | Text: "Nothing to do for the next five minutes." |
| 24–32 | Return to keys on tray, aligning seamlessly with first-frame action for loop. | Tiny corner: "Xoot • Your home. Handled." |

**No before/after and no false footage of a cleaner.** Use authored/rights-cleared foley and music only, no repurposed watermarked video. **Measure:** replay/completion and saves against spoken Reel control. **Production warning:** hands with multiple objects are a diffusion artefact hotspot; prefer cuts into isolated simple actions.

## Production and measurement runbook

### Sequence — Xoot launch readiness first
1. Verify current payment flow, domain resolution, launch suburbs, bookings, operational capacity, safe claims and editorial brand references. **Prelaunch material may be produced and tested as brand entertainment before product readiness only if there is no booking CTA or implied availability.**
2. Founder signs off on one character reference, one approved synthetic voice, disclosure treatment and all five scripts. Voice should be original and South African, not a Grace Mondlana soundalike.
3. Make a 4–6 s character continuity proof (skin pattern, hair, wardrobe, earrings, board/knife proportions and head motion). Only after QA create all shots; use the same reference for every cut.
4. Composite narration, foley and captions after scene generation rather than asking one model to perfectly voice a long monologue. In the video editing tool assemble 5–10 second shots with J/L cuts, preserve natural pauses, trim dead air, proofread every text overlay.
5. Review visually at 25/50/100% playback: continuity, anomalies, voice/sync, no misleading real-world service proof, AI marker visibility, licensed sound, subtitles, sound levels, caption safe areas. Human approves exact posted asset.
6. Publish as labelled Xoot creative using a consistent series identity; repurpose Reels to TikTok, or reverse, without creating a second production pipeline unnecessarily. Observe metrics and comments, never auto-publish without approval.

### Experiment register

| Video | Primary behavioural hypothesis | Primary signal at 72 h | Secondary signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Vlog | relatability holds attention | median/average watch time | non-follower reach, local profile visits, comments |
| 02 Chopping | parallel action supports story retention | 3-s hold vs rest; average watch time | comments, watch completion |
| 03 Skit | humour gets sent | shares/sends per reach | local follower/profile conversion |
| 04 Tutorial | practical help gets saved | saves per reach | replay and profile conversion |
| 05 ASMR | sensory loop lifts completion | completion/replays | saves, follows |

**Commercial scoreboard remains:** profile visits per 1,000 impressions and then verified local enquiries/bookings once the funnel is ready; channel likes/views alone are not success. Segment South Africa / intended operating suburbs as available; raw global virality may be commercially useless.

**Method:** Post a baseline of the five formats, one at a time, without paid boosting; record length, first-3-second hold, average watch time, total watch, shares, saves, comments, impressions, non-follower reach, local profile visits, and (later) qualified enquiries. Where eligible use Instagram Trial Reels for format experiments with non-followers. Do not infer statistical superiority from five non-random posts. In the second cycle, remake the top 2 formats changing **one** thing (hook OR duration OR story topic), retaining the same character/edit quality; keep the other three as exploratory candidates.

**Seasonal scheduling:** Do not advertise a January 2027 Bryanston pilot or February public launch as committed customer availability until operational approval verifies it. Avoid geographies outside staffed coverage. Future-leaning content is safest when it is lifestyle observation rather than launch claims.
