# The Xoot AI presenter — character brief

**Persona 01.** Written 2026-09-02 from a founder-supplied reference image.

## ⚠️ Why this document has to be this detailed

**The generator cannot see the reference image.** Higgsfield's tooling cannot read attachments from
a Claude conversation; media must arrive as a URL or an in-app upload, and a chat attachment is
neither. I can see the image. The model cannot.

**So this brief is the image.** Every detail that matters has to survive as text, because "match the
reference" is an instruction nothing downstream can follow.

---

## What she is, and the three lines she never crosses

She is a **brand presenter**. A character who hosts, narrates and demonstrates.

### ⚠️ Founder decision, 2026-09-02 — she speaks in first person, and that is why she is illustrated

The founder asked that she be able to **talk about her experience using Xoot**. That is a normal
brand-character line — and it is only normal while nobody could mistake her for a real person.

**The two settings are locked together, and it is worth understanding why rather than obeying it.**
A cartoon tiger saying "they're great" is advertising; nobody reads it as a customer's account. A
*photorealistic* woman on a real Cape Town street saying a cleaner came to her home is a testimonial
— and with **zero completed bookings**, it would describe a service nobody has received. Under **CPA
s41** a misleading representation is misleading regardless of intent, and an AI-disclosure label does
not cure it, because the claim being made is not *"I am real"* but *"this happened"*.

**So the visual direction changed rather than the script.** She is now unmistakably an illustration,
and in exchange she may speak as a brand character in the first person.

| She may | She may never |
|---|---|
| Present, host, narrate, demonstrate the product | Be rendered photorealistically, or be mistakable for a photograph |
| **Speak in the first person as a brand character** — "I love how fast this books" | Be presented as a real, specific customer, named or implied |
| Express warmth and enthusiasm about Xoot | Give a star rating, a numeric review, or a before/after account |
| Appear in lifestyle scenes and deliver factual product lines | Appear as a Xooter, or as a member of staff |
| Be consistent and recognisable across the library | Describe an outcome the product cannot deliver today |

⚠️ **The last row is the one that will get missed.** "I love how fast this books" is fine — booking
is fast. "My Xooter arrived in ten minutes" is not, because nothing guarantees that. **She may be
enthusiastic about things that are true; she may not invent specifics.**

**Every published asset still carries an AI-generated marker.** It is required by Meta and TikTok,
and it is the belt to the illustration's braces.

This is a brand asking women to let a stranger into their home, so the disclosure is not a
formality. The ARB code treats a testimonial as requiring a real, identifiable person, and
`BRAND_GUIDE` independently bans *"fake reviews, invented credentials"* — **which is exactly why the
illustrated route is the one that lets her speak at all.**

## The specification

### Person

- South African woman, mid-to-late twenties
- Athletic-slim, average height, natural proportions — **not a fashion-model silhouette**
- Long dark brown wavy hair, centre-parted, loose and slightly windblown, past the shoulder
- Warm mid-brown skin
- Softly defined brows, minimal makeup, no gloss or glam retouching
- Composed, level, unsmiling default expression. **Self-possessed rather than friendly** — she is not
  performing delight at a cleaning product
- Sunglasses pushed up onto the crown of the head as a recurring signature
- Small gold hoop earrings

### ⚠️ Vitiligo — the consistency anchor, and a deliberate choice

The reference carries **vitiligo**: irregular depigmented patches across the cheeks and chin, the
throat and collarbone, both forearms, and the midriff. Soft-edged, asymmetric, organic in outline.

**This is the single most valuable feature for a trained Soul.** It is what makes her unmistakably
one character across every future asset, in a way hair or clothing never can — those change; a skin
pattern does not.

**It is also a choice worth recording rather than copying.** On a real person, visible vitiligo reads
as representation. On a synthetic person it can read as costume if handled carelessly.

**The rule: keep it, and never mention it.** It is never referenced in copy, never a subject, never
framed as a story. She simply has it, the way a real person does. The moment it becomes a talking
point it stops being a face and starts being a device.

### Wardrobe — the signature look

- **Fitted graphic baby tee.** Y2K/vintage-rock print: washed greys, muted reds, faded black.
  Cropped just above the natural waist, cap sleeves, distressed print texture
- **Oversized khaki cargo trousers.** Low-slung, wide leg, thigh cargo pockets, drawstring hems
  puddling over the shoe
- **White leather low-top trainers**, box-fresh
- **Two chunky silver curb chains** at different lengths
- **Small tan-and-brown shoulder bag** on a short strap, worn high under the arm

⚠️ **Generated garment graphics produce garbled text.** The reference has it — the shirt reads
"R.TRADY", which is not a word. **Frame so print text is small, obscured, or out of focus**, or use
a wordless graphic. A legible nonsense word on a brand asset is the tell that undoes the rest.

### Environment

**The point of the whole direction, and where the category is beatable.** Every cleaning brand shoots
an empty white kitchen. This does the opposite.

- Real urban South African street: a crossing, a weathered mid-rise, exterior aircon units, tangled
  overhead cabling, a market awning, painted zebra stripes underfoot
- **Real passers-by in frame, mid-motion, not looking at camera.** They make it a photograph rather
  than a set
- Lived-in, not derelict. Ordinary, not picturesque

For interior work: a real-feeling Cape Town or Johannesburg flat — occupied, slightly cluttered, with
possessions in it. **Never a showroom.**

### Render style — illustrated, and this is the load-bearing decision

**Stylised editorial illustration.** Flat-to-semi-flat vector-adjacent shapes, confident line, warm
limited palette, visible grain or paper texture. The register of a good brand illustration, not a
cartoon and not a render.

**Why not the workflow's off-the-shelf presets.** Higgsfield's `character-sheet` workflow offers
`anime-2d`, `3d-stylized` and `game-concept`. All three are unmistakably not-real, which satisfies
the constraint — but `3d-stylized` is Pixar-adjacent and reads as childlike, which collides with
*premium managed home-care infrastructure*, and `anime-2d` is a subculture signal this audience did
not ask for. The workflow explicitly allows a custom module in the same shape, and this is one.

⚠️ **The line to hold: stylised, never nearly-photoreal.** The failure mode is drifting toward
render-realism one asset at a time until she is back where she started. **If a frame could be
mistaken for a photograph at thumbnail size, it is rejected** — that is the whole basis on which she
is allowed to speak in the first person.

**What survives from the founder's reference, unchanged.** This is the point worth noticing: almost
everything distinctive about that image was never the photorealism.

- The **wardrobe** — Y2K graphic tee, oversized cargos, chunky chains, white low-tops
- The **street setting** — crossing, weathered mid-rise, aircon units, cables, awning, passers-by
- The **anti-glamour framing** — flat-on, eye-level, full-length, centred, subject in the middle third
- The **vitiligo**, which is still the consistency anchor and works even better in flat colour
- The **flat overcast light**, now as even ambient shading rather than diffused daylight

**Only the photorealism goes.** That was the liability, and it was never the edge — the edge was
being visually unlike every empty-white-kitchen cleaning ad in the category.

### Composition

- Flat-on, eye-level, centred, full-length, subject in the middle third
- Even ambient shading, no rim light, no dramatic key
- Clean silhouette that reads at thumbnail size — the format is a 9:16 phone screen

### Palette

Khaki, warm grey, washed black, off-white, muted brick red, weathered concrete, silver hardware.
It already sits close to the Xoot design kit, which is why the direction works.

## Prompt skeleton

Reused across the character sheet and every later asset, so drift has one place to creep in.

> Stylised editorial illustration, flat semi-flat vector-adjacent shapes with confident linework,
> warm limited palette, subtle paper grain, clearly illustrated and not photographic. Identical
> original South African woman in her late twenties, mid-brown skin with soft irregular vitiligo
> patches across cheeks, chin, throat, forearms and midriff rendered as clean flat shapes. Long dark
> brown wavy centre-parted hair, sunglasses pushed up on her head, small gold hoops, two chunky
> silver curb chains. Fitted cropped vintage-rock graphic tee in washed grey and muted red,
> oversized khaki cargo trousers with drawstring hems, white low-top trainers, small tan shoulder
> bag. Standing on an urban street crossing, weathered mid-rise buildings, exterior aircon units,
> overhead cables, market awning, simplified passers-by. Even ambient shading, flat-on, eye-level,
> centred, full length, clean silhouette. Composed warm expression.

**Negative direction:** photorealistic, photograph, 3D render, subsurface scattering, skin pores,
studio lighting, shallow depth of field, glamour retouching, empty white interiors, legible garment
text, stock-photo staging, Pixar, anime.

⚠️ **`photorealistic` and `photograph` lead the negative list deliberately.** They are the two words
standing between a brand character and a fabricated testimonial.

## Production route

**Reusable Soul, not one-off generations.** Consistency across a library that will grow is the entire
value; one-off `soul_cast` output drifts between assets and defeats the purpose.

| Step | Tool | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `get_workflow_instructions({workflow: "character-sheet"})` | Load before generating — its own tooling says so |
| 2 | `generate_image_batch`, 8–12 frames | Varied angle, expression, framing. Same palette and shading |

⚠️ **The model choice changed with the style.** `soul_cast` and `soul_2` are built for
identity-faithful *photoreal* people, which is now the opposite of what is wanted. Call
`models_explore(action:'recommend')` with an illustration goal before generating, and expect the
answer to be a general image model rather than the Soul pipeline. **If the chosen model has no Soul
equivalent, consistency comes from a locked prompt skeleton plus reference elements instead of a
trained identity** — a different mechanism for the same result, and the registry records whichever
is used.
| 3 | **Founder reviews the sheet** | The last checkpoint before credits become an identity |
| 4 | `show_characters(action='train')` | Soul training, 5–20 inputs |
| 5 | Record `soul_id` in `personas/01-*.md` | A Soul whose id is unrecorded cannot be reused |
| 6 | `soul_2` + `soul_id` for every later asset | — |

**`get_cost` before every batch.** `use_unlim` is left unset so the server asks rather than silently
choosing which balance to spend.
