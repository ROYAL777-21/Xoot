# Partnership proposal — cleaning and hospitality training schools

Written 2026-09-02. A template to send, plus the reasoning behind every clause so you can defend it
in the room and adapt it without breaking it.

---

## Why this is the right move, and why now

`launch-protocol.md` gate 5 says **supply before demand**, and calls it the one gate that looks
compressible and is not. Demand arriving in a suburb with no Xooter coverage does not convert — it
produces cancellations and one-star reviews, and in a market this small those are permanent.

A training school is the only supply channel that arrives **pre-vetted, pre-trained and in cohorts**
rather than one applicant at a time. It also solves the thing that makes cold recruitment expensive:
you are not persuading someone to try a new kind of work, you are meeting people who have already
paid to enter the industry and now need somewhere to work.

**What you have that they want:** their placement rate is the number they market with. Most training
providers can teach, and cannot place. That is the trade.

## The proposal — send this

> **Subject: Placement partnership — guaranteed first-booking pathway for your graduates**
>
> Dear [NAME],
>
> I am [FOUNDER NAME], founder of Xoot, a home-care platform operating in [SUBURBS].
>
> I am writing with a straightforward proposal: **we would like to become a placement destination for
> your graduates**, and we are willing to commit to it in writing rather than in principle.
>
> **What we would offer your graduates**
>
> 1. **A fast-tracked application.** Graduates of [SCHOOL] who present a certificate skip the general
>    queue and go straight to our vetting review — ID, references and banking checks — rather than
>    waiting behind unscreened applications.
> 2. **Recognition of your training.** Our onboarding covers cleaning standards and chemical safety,
>    hospitality and communication, child-safety essentials, eco-friendly practice and garden work.
>    Where your curriculum already covers a module, we will credit it rather than making your
>    graduate sit it twice. We would want to review your syllabus once to map this properly.
> 3. **Earnings that rise with proven work.** Xooters keep 65% of every booking's value from their
>    first job, rising to 80% as completed jobs and reliability accumulate. The ladder is published
>    in the app, so your graduate can see exactly what the next rung requires.
> 4. **Work near where they live.** We match bookings by suburb proximity. Travel between jobs is
>    the largest unpaid cost your graduates will face, and reducing it is a design goal of our
>    matching engine.
>
> **What we would ask of [SCHOOL]**
>
> 1. Introduce Xoot to each graduating cohort — a short session we can deliver, or material you
>    hand out, whichever you prefer.
> 2. Confirm certificates when we ask, so we can honour the fast-track without being defrauded.
> 3. Tell us honestly which graduates you would and would not recommend. This is the most valuable
>    thing in the arrangement and the reason it beats advertising.
>
> **What we would not ask**
>
> - No exclusivity. Your graduates should work wherever suits them, and a partnership that limits
>   their options is not one you should sign.
> - No fee from you. We are not selling you anything.
> - No commitment on numbers from either side while we are both learning whether this works.
>
> **What we would like to start with**
>
> One cohort. [NUMBER] graduates, tracked properly: how many apply, how many activate, how many
> complete a first booking, and what they earn. We will share those numbers with you whether they
> are flattering or not, because a partnership built on a figure neither of us checked is not worth
> renewing.
>
> If placement outcomes are something you report to students or to accreditation, we can provide
> them in a form you can use.
>
> Could we meet for thirty minutes in the next two weeks? I am happy to come to you.
>
> [FOUNDER NAME]
> [ROLE], Xoot
> [PHONE] · [EMAIL] · xoot.co.za

## Notes for the meeting

### Their first question will be "how much work can you actually give them?"

**Answer it honestly, and early.** You are pre-launch with low booking volume. A school that places
graduates into no work will stop sending them and tell others.

The honest and still-attractive version: *"Not much yet, and I would rather tell you that now than
have your graduates find out. What I can commit to is that the first cohort gets first access as
demand arrives, and that I will show you the real numbers either way."* A partner who trusts your
figures is worth more than a partner you flattered once.

### Their second question will be about pay

Have the ladder ready and quote it exactly: 65% at New, 67.5% at 5 completed jobs, 70% at 15, 75% at
50, 80% at 120, with a reliability score gate at each rung. On a R250 four-hour clean that is R162.50
to the Xooter at New and R200 at Platinum.

**Do not quote a monthly figure.** There is no volume to support one, and under CPA s41 a
representation is misleading regardless of intent.

### What to actually ask them for, beyond graduates

Training schools know things you do not, and this is the cheaper half of the meeting:

- Which suburbs their students travel from — that is a real supply map for gate 5
- What the going rate is at their other placement partners
- Why graduates leave the industry, which is your retention problem before it is your recruitment one

### ⛔ Settle the classification question before signing anything

`xooter-hiring-ad.md` explains this in full and it applies here with more force. **A training school
will ask whether their graduates become employees.** Under **LRA s200A** a person earning below the
BCEA threshold is presumed to be an employee if any of seven factors applies. Nothing in this
repository defines the relationship. Get an attorney's answer before you describe it to an
institution that will repeat your description to its students.

### One thing to prepare that is not in the letter

Bring a Xooter, if you have one who has completed real bookings. A graduate hears one person who has
done the work over any number of slides. If you do not have one yet, that is itself a signal that
this meeting is a month early — the strongest version of this pitch is made after gate 6, once
10–20 friendly bookings exist and you can describe them.

## Targets worth approaching first

Not researched here — this is the shape of the shortlist, and the marketing agents can populate it:

1. **Accredited providers with a Services SETA cleaning or hygiene qualification.** Accreditation
   means the syllabus is documented and mappable to your modules.
2. **Hospitality and housekeeping academies.** Hotel housekeeping training transfers directly to
   Airbnb turnovers, which is one of your named service types.
3. **NPO and community skills programmes in your launch suburbs.** Highest alignment with proximity
   matching, because their intake already lives where the work is.

⚠️ Fill this in with real, checked names before sending anything. A proposal addressed to a school
whose curriculum you have not read reads exactly like a mail merge, which is how it will be treated.
