# Personal branding plan — `@TheRealSkosana`

The **sequence**. What to do, in what order, and what has to be true before the next step.

The strategy behind it is `founder-brand-strategy.md` (villain, pillars, register); the evidence is
`build-in-public-accounts.md` and `content-strategy-references.md`. This document does not restate
them — it orders them.

**Starting position, measured 2026-09-01:** 1 follower, 32 posts, following 23. No baseline. Xoot's
company accounts do not exist. That is phase zero, and the plan is built for it rather than for the
account you want to have.

---

## Phase 0 — before a single post. This week.

Nothing here is content. All of it gates content.

### 0.1 Rewrite the bio ⛔ **this is the whole gate**

The 3-second test: a visitor must learn **who you are, what you do, and what they get** before they
scroll on.

| Account | Bio | |
|---|---|---|
| `@levelsio` | products with MRR beside each — `$80K/m`, `$23K/m`, `$20K/m` | ✅ the proof *is* the bio |
| `@jacobrodri_` | "founder [appkittie] — find winning apps, replicate what's working" | ✅ who / what / what-you-get, 14 words |
| `@TheRealSkosana` | three jokes | ❌ never says a company exists |

The jokes are not the problem; the **absence** is. At 1 follower, no amount of good content
compensates for a profile that does not say what you do. This is one hour and it gates everything
after it.

**Shape to write to** — not copy: *what you are building* + *for whom* + *what a reader gets from
following*. Keep one line of the humour if you want it; add the two lines that are missing.

### 0.2 Answer the three questions only you can answer

From `founder-brand-strategy.md` §2. What is the bane of your existence; what was the lowest point;
what is the worst version of yourself. **Every answer is also a post**, and they are the specificity
that no model can invent — which is the AI-detection defence and the trust mechanism at once.

### 0.3 Pick the medium, then the platform — in that order

Ralston's ordering, and the reason matters: picking a platform first commits you to a medium you may
not sustain. Four mediums — video, audio, written, graphic. Pick the one you would still be doing in
six months on a bad week.

**Solo means one primary + one secondary platform.** Not four. The primary gets the pillar content
and all the innovation; the secondary gets repurposed work.

> Given the material this repo generates — defects, decisions, numbers — **written on X as primary**
> is the honest default, with LinkedIn secondary. But this is your call and it should follow 0.3,
> not precede it.

### 0.4 Set the cadence against the capacity you will have in six months

Not this week's capacity. Volume is easy to add and very hard to walk back once an audience expects
it, and you are about to get busier, not less busy. **73% of people who start making content quit
within the first year** — almost always on cadence, not on ideas.

## Phase 1 — weeks 1 to 4. Expansion, and no thresholds.

### What runs

The **week-one set of 7** already exists on the Eden Content Engine board: three formats × two topics
plus one thread, so a result tells you whether the *format* or the *topic* caused it. Weeks 2–4
continue at the same shape.

### ⚠️ Why there are no targets in this phase

A pre-registered threshold is the thing that stops a result being narrated as a win afterwards — and
it is meaningless without a baseline. Yours does not exist. "Aim for 2% engagement" at 1 follower is
an invented number graded against itself.

**So phase 1 measures. Phase 2 tests.** This is the accordion's expansion half: use volume to find
out what your audience calls quality, because quality is defined by them and not by you.

### The four numbers, per post, at 72 hours

**Impressions** (the denominator) · **profile visits** (the real conversion at this stage) ·
**replies** (the only unambiguously human signal) · **saves** (the signal that it had *value*).

Not followers — moves in ones and twos and attributes to nothing. Not likes as a primary — politeness,
not usefulness.

## Phase 2 — weeks 5 to 12. Contract, then iterate.

**Thresholds now come from phase-1 data**, which is the first point at which they mean anything.

**Contract the accordion:** put the same total effort into fewer pieces, concentrated on what phase 1
showed the audience responding to.

**Then 70 / 20 / 10:**

- **70%** — what you now know works.
- **20%** — iterations changing **one variable** (hook, opening, length, packaging). One at a time,
  or you learn nothing from either.
- **10%** — big swings. This is the one people abandon once they have a baseline to protect, and it
  is the one that finds your next format *before* the audience fatigues on the current one.

**Rank on profile visits per 1,000 impressions.** Never reach. Five accounts measured, and on every
one the highest-reach post had the worst engagement rate — levelsio's politics post took 26.6M views
at 0.06% while his numbered rules list took 615K at 2.36%.

---

## Phase 3 — quarter two. Rotate.

Move the innovation to the secondary platform; the primary drops to maintenance mode running formats
already proven. Come back to the primary when the audience fatigues — they will.

## The per-draft checks, in one place

Every draft, before it goes anywhere:

1. **Value** — can a stranger who will never book a cleaner do something differently after reading it?
2. **Payload in sentence one** — or does the reader have to wait for it?
3. **Contextual credibility** — not "I build software" but the specific thing that makes you worth
   hearing on *this* subject.
4. **Specificity** — a number, a command, an error message that could not have been invented.
5. **One idea**, no hedging, and could it lose a third of its words?
6. **Present tense is capped at what is true today.** Anything behind a readiness constant does not
   exist publicly. Future tense is unlimited.
