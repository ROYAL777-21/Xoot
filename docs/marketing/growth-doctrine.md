# Xoot growth doctrine

**Source.** Condensed from the founder's Master Business Reference Document (Hormozi: $100M
Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models, and the Pricing, Price-Raise, Retention, Lead-Nurture,
Marketing-Machine and Hooks playbooks), plus the AEO brief of 2026-08-26. This file is the
applied form — the frameworks as Xoot must use them, with the places they collide with South
African law or with this product called out.

**Precedence, when two of these disagree:**

1. South African law — the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 above all.
2. `docs/product/locked-commercial-facts.md` and the live pricing ladder.
3. This doctrine.
4. Anything retrieved from the `ask-hormozi` corpus.

A framework never authorises a claim the product cannot honour. Under s41 a misleading
representation is unlawful **regardless of intent**, and under s30 you may not present a price
as a reduction from one never actually charged. Several standard growth tactics below are
therefore constrained, and where they are, it says so.

---

## 1. The three levers, and nothing else

A business grows exactly three ways: **more customers, higher average purchase value, more
purchases per customer.** Every marketing activity is a subcategory. If a proposal does not
name which lever it moves, it is not a proposal.

For Xoot there is a fourth constraint that is not a growth lever but caps all three:
**Xooter supply.** Demand that outruns coverage produces cancellations, refunds and one-star
reviews — growth spend converted into reputational damage.

## 2. The value equation

`Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort)`

The lever most businesses ignore is the denominator, and it is the one Xoot is strongest on.

| Term | What it is for Xoot | How to move it |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Coming home to it done. The mental load put down. | Write the moment, not the service |
| Perceived likelihood | "Will she actually show up, and is she safe in my home?" | Vetting detail, ratings, same-Xooter continuity, guarantees |
| Time delay | Booking → clean home | Faster matching, clear arrival windows |
| Effort | Briefing, being home, trusting a stranger | Saved household profiles, recurring schedules, continuity |

**Perceived likelihood is the binding term in this market.** A South African woman booking a
stranger into her home is not primarily price-sensitive; she is risk-sensitive. Marketing that
competes on price while leaving the safety question unanswered is optimising the wrong variable.

## 3. Offer construction

Identify the dream outcome → list every problem on the way → convert each to a solution →
choose delivery vehicles → trim low-value/high-cost and stack the rest.

**Enhancers, and their legal limits here:**

- **Scarcity** — only when true. "5 spots left" when there are not is an s41 misrepresentation.
  Xoot has *genuine* scarcity: Xooter availability per suburb per weekend. Use the real one.
- **Urgency** — a real deadline, honoured. If the price does not actually rise, do not say it will.
- **Bonuses over discounts.** Discounting teaches customers the price is negotiable and, under
  s30, a struck-through price that was never charged is unlawful. Adding value is both stronger
  and safer.
- **Guarantees** — the highest-leverage and highest-risk item on this list. Only offer what
  operations will honour every time, and write it where support can see it. A guarantee the
  company quietly declines is worse than none.

**Naming (M-A-G-I-C):** Magnetic reason, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container. When an offer
fatigues, change the wrapper, not the operations.

## 4. Leads: the core four

1-to-1 warm · 1-to-many warm (content) · 1-to-1 cold · 1-to-many cold (paid).

At Xoot's stage, in order of expected return:

1. **Content to a warm-ish local audience** — the blog and social programme. Compounds, cheap,
   and the only one that also produces AI citations.
2. **Xooter recruitment content** — relieves the supply constraint, which is the actual ceiling.
3. **Warm outreach** — the founder's own network for the first customers and first reviews.
4. **Paid** — last, and only once the funnel converts. Paid traffic into a funnel that does not
   convert buys a precise measurement of how bad the funnel is, at retail price.

**Give before asking.** At most one ask per five posts.

## 5. Money model

Cover acquisition cost inside 30 days so growth is self-funding. Target **LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1**.

Applicable to Xoot: attraction (first-clean offer), upsell (Care+ at the right moment — after a
good first clean, never during checkout), continuity (Care+ itself, and recurring schedules),
downsell (a smaller clean rather than a discounted one — change features, never negotiate price).

**Do not adopt "win your money back", giveaways or trial-with-penalty without legal review.**
Prize competitions and conditional refunds sit inside the CPA's promotional-competition rules;
getting them wrong is a regulatory problem, not a growth experiment.

## 6. Pricing

Value-based, never competitor-matched. Raising price is the strongest single lever — it lifts
revenue per customer *and* cuts delivery cost by lowering volume. The virtuous cycle is real:
higher price → higher commitment → better outcomes → less churn.

**Xoot-specific constraint: there is a legal floor.** The R47/hr and R54/hr floors exist for
the Xooter's sake and are not a pricing variable. Price moves up from there, never down through
it. Any pricing change goes through a decision-log entry — never change a number in code and
leave `locked-commercial-facts.md` saying something else.

## 7. Retention

The leaky bucket caps everything. Find the activation behaviour, drive new customers to it,
build community and continuity, offer annual terms, and run exit interviews before the cancel
completes — they save roughly half.

**Same-Xooter continuity is the strongest retention asset this product has.** People do not
leave people.

## 8. Hooks

Call-out + condition for value. Allocate **70% proven / 20% adjacent / 10% new**. Never a hook
the content does not pay off — an overpromising headline is the same s41 exposure as an
overpromising price, and readers punish it immediately.

## 9. AEO — being the answer, not the link

Assistants fan one prompt out into many sub-searches, so partial coverage loses. Citations are
driven by **consensus** (appearing in many places), **freshness**, and **authority** (classic
SEO still underneath).

What this changes: own topics completely rather than chasing single keywords; write atomic,
declarative, entity-rich passages that lift cleanly; put the answer first (BLUF); keep pages
genuinely current; and earn third-party mentions, because a branded mention elsewhere is a
stronger AI-visibility signal than anything you publish about yourself.

**Check the crawlers are not blocked before spending a cent on any of it.**

## 10. Measurement

Every activity declares its metric before it starts. See `docs/marketing/measurement-spec.md`.
An experiment without a pre-registered success criterion is a story told afterwards.
