### ⚠️ Why `@jacobrodri_` is the most important of the three, and Musk the least usable

Musk and Hormozi are being used here as *technique* references, which is correct. But there is a
structural problem with Musk that has to be said once and then designed around:

**His compression works because 241.5 million people already know who he is.** "the bird is freed"
is a 89.76× outlier *from the man who had just bought the company*. The same four words from an
account with two hundred followers is not a post — it is noise with no referent. His median top-ten
post is four words because he has already spent twenty years earning the context those four words
sit inside.

`@jacobrodri_` is at ~21.6K followers, started September 2024, and is building in public about
growth for a product he owns. **That is a phase `@TheRealSkosana` can actually copy** — the cadence,
the post shapes, the ratio of teaching to shipping, how a small account earns attention without
borrowed authority. Take *structure* from Musk, *density* from Hormozi, and **phase behaviour from
Jacob Rodri.**

The one Musk form that does transfer immediately is the forward-tense optimist post — "The future is
gonna be fantastic", one of only two non-political posts in his top ten, and exactly the founder's
stated register. That one, copy directly.

## 1. The world — villain, vision, main character

*Frame from the 90-day video. Applied.*

### The villain

**The informal cash arrangement for domestic work.** Unvetted, unrecorded, unprotected, and quietly
awkward on both sides. Nobody chose it — it is what everybody inherited. It fails the customer (no
vetting, no recourse, no record) and it fails the worker harder (no floor on pay, no reliability
history that travels, no way to convert five years of good work into more or better-paid work).

This is nameable without naming a competitor and without punching down at any person, which is the
only safe form of villain for a brand whose workers and customers sit on both sides of every South
African line. **The villain is a system. Never a person.**

### The vision

**Domestic work as a real job with a real record.** Reliability measured, portable, and worth money
to the person who earned it. A home entered by someone whose history is visible. The price shown is
the price charged. None of that sounds futuristic — and none of it is how the market works today,
which is what makes it a forward claim rather than a boast.

### The main character — and the sequencing that follows

**Season one is the Xooter, not the customer.** Supply is the ceiling: demand arriving in a suburb
with no Xooter coverage converts into cancellations and one-star reviews, which is the fastest way
to waste a launch. Recruitment content is simultaneously the cheapest acquisition channel Xoot owns
*and* the one that relieves the actual binding constraint. Season two turns to households.

### The skill tree — what a reader has to come to believe, in order

1. What you pay for home services is knowable and does not have to be negotiated at the door.
2. A worker having a measured record is worth paying for — and worth *being*.
3. "We vetted them" is a claim. It should arrive with evidence attached.
4. ⛔ Software built for South African households can be as good as software built anywhere.
   *This is closest to your futurist register and I want your wording, not mine.*

### The vocabulary

`Xooter` already works — it names an identity, not a task. Candidates for the rest:

- **The record** — the reliability history a Xooter accumulates. Already real in code
  (`src/lib/xooter-commission.ts` moves actual money on it), which is what stops it being a slogan.
- **The shown price** — CPA s41 turned into a brand promise. This repo has shipped a screen that
  displayed one number and charged another **three times**; the guard test that now stops it is why
  the phrase means something.
- ⛔ **A name for the villain.** The named enemy is the piece people repeat back to you, so it has to
  come out of your mouth, not mine.

## 3. The pillars — PESTO, mapped onto Xoot

*Marcos Ruiz's five pillars, translated. Roughly: 2 Expertise, 1 Social proof, 1 Personal,
1 Opinion per week, with Trending taken only when it genuinely fits.*

| Pillar | Xoot's version | Why it earns |
|---|---|---|
| **Expertise** | The build. What shipped, what broke, what it cost. | This repo produces it weekly without being asked. It is the only pillar where you have a real supply advantage over every other founder in this market. |
| **Social proof** | The record. Real Xooter outcomes, real bookings, real numbers — once they exist. | ⚠️ Currently thin. You have ~0 completed bookings. **Do not manufacture this pillar**; run it at low volume until it is real. |
| **Personal** | The three ⛔ answers above, and the founder's own week. | The AI-detection defence and the reason anyone follows a person rather than a company. |
| **Opinions** | A specific technical or commercial position a peer could argue with. | The narrow, defensible form of "occasionally controversial". Never race, religion, party politics, load-shedding blame, or immigration — Xooters and customers sit across all of those, and a hot take costs *them* income. |
| **Trending** | Take it only when the trend actually intersects the work. | Forcing it is the fastest way to sound like every other account. |

## 4. The method — how a post gets made

*Craig Perry's process, compressed to the four steps that matter.*

**1. Validated idea × favourite idea.** *"Find a way to connect validated ideas with your favourite
ideas."* The validated packaging is a proven founder-post shape. The favourite idea is a real defect
in a real payments system in a real emerging market. **Nobody else on X is publishing that**, which
is exactly the intersection the method is looking for.

**2. Broad opening, narrow body.** Get people through the door, then explore the house. The named
failure mode is the esoteric hook on a genuinely good idea.

**3. The outline is 80–90% of the work.** Problem → insight → solution, with the favourite idea in
the insight slot. Write nothing until every part of the outline is ticked.

**4. One 1% improvement a week.** Take one post you admire from the Swipe File. Work out what every
sentence is doing at the mechanism level. Extract **one** principle. Apply it that week. That is the
whole improvement loop, and it costs an hour.

### The scoring checklist, applied to every draft

| Check | Source | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Brevity | Musk | Could it lose a third of its words and lose nothing? |
| Specificity | Hormozi | Is there a number, name or detail a model could not invent? |
| One idea | Both | One point, or three at half strength? |
| No hedging | Musk | "I think maybe it could be" — cut it or commit to it |
| **Payload in sentence one** | Ben Meer | Or does the reader have to wait for the value? |
| **Time-priced, where relevant** | Hormozi | "in 90 Mins" is 3 of his top 12 titles |

## 5. Phase — where this account actually is

*Marcos Ruiz's phase model, applied honestly.*

Under 5K followers, ≥50% of posts should be top-of-funnel. `@TheRealSkosana`'s count is unknown to me
and Xoot's company accounts **do not exist**. That is not phase one, it is **phase zero**, and the
first job is not reach — it is having somewhere for reach to land.

Which is the same argument as instrumenting before optimising, and the same argument as clearing the
Phase 0 gates (GitHub billing, Netlify link, PayFast credentials) before spending a rand on
distribution. Launch traffic into a site that cannot take a booking and you buy a 404.

**The 3-second credibility test** applies now and costs nothing: a visitor to the profile must learn
who you are, what you do, and what you can do for them in three seconds. Bio, pinned post, header.
That is a one-hour job and it gates everything downstream.

### Bottleneck analysis — the metric that governs the loop

impressions → profile visits → followers → conversations → bookings. Work only on the worst ratio.
**Unusable until Phase 1 instrumentation exists**, because every one of those ratios is currently
unmeasured. Do not run the loop on vibes; run the 1%-per-week loop instead until the numbers arrive.

## 6. The rule that governs everything above

Optimism about the future is unlimited. **Optimism about the present is capped at what is true
today.** In eight days this codebase produced nine defects where a document asserted something
worked and it did not. Anything behind a readiness constant (`TIP_READINESS`, `SAFETY_READINESS`,
`canOfferTip`, `canArmSos`) does not exist publicly.

"We're building toward X" ✅ — "We have X" when X is gated ❌. That second one is both a CPA s41
exposure and precisely the credibility loss that building in public is meant to buy you.
