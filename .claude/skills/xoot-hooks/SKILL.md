# Xoot hooks and format validation

## The hook is most of the spend

Once the headline is written, most of the budget is committed. A hook has two parts:

- **The call-out** — selects the right person. "Sandton moms", a visual of a specific kind of
  kitchen, a sound. Can be non-verbal.
- **The condition for value** — promises them something specific. "here's what a deep clean
  should actually cost."

A hook with a call-out and no value promise is noise. A hook with value and no call-out reaches
the wrong people efficiently.

## Types worth rotating

Label · yes-question · open question · conditional · command · statement · list/steps · narrative.

Rotate deliberately — consecutive posts using the same type is the fastest route to a feed that
reads as templated, and it is the thing both audiences and detectors notice.

## 70 / 20 / 10

- **70% proven** — patterns already winning here or demonstrably elsewhere.
- **20% adjacent** — variations on a current winner.
- **10% new** — genuinely untested, expected to fail. This is where next quarter's winner comes
  from, so it is not optional. It is also not the majority.

## Format validation — the numbers that decide scale

Validate the **format** separately from the **content**. A winning format transfers across topics;
a winning topic rarely transfers across formats. Track them as two variables and you learn twice
per test.

| Gate | Threshold | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **3-second retention** | **75–80%** | The single best predictor. Below it, the hook is broken — fix the hook, not the video |
| Engagement rate | >12% | Viral candidate |
| Outperformance | 3–5× account average | Boost candidate |
| Convertability | Comments asking *where do I get this* | The only comment that predicts installs |

**The View Trap.** High views with low engagement is clickbait that did not deliver. It reads as
success and converts nobody. **Track engagement, never views alone.** A video with 8k views and
real comments beats one with 200k and none.

**Proof the content is doing the work:** a format that performs from an account with few followers
proves the *content* works rather than the audience. That is the format worth copying.

## Structure that works here

**Hook → demo.** A strong opening paired with an immediate, authentic demonstration. Do not
explain before showing.

Note for any future AI-generated video: models remain weakest at a person speaking, and the
uncanny result attracts hostile comments that poison the post. Favour **text-on-screen** and
**demonstration** formats over talking heads.

## Xoot-specific hook material

The real objections, in the order South Africans raise them:

1. **A stranger in my home.** The first objection for most women, and it is not hypothetical.
   Do not talk around it and do not be breezy. Say what the vetting actually is.
2. **Will she show up.**
3. **What does it actually cost, and what am I getting.**
4. **I should be doing this myself.** Answered with permission, never with shame.

Hooks that consistently earn attention here: the specific cost of not knowing; the question they
have already typed; the local dated observation (Highveld dust in August, month-end moving,
pre-Christmas deep cleans, load-shedding cleanup).

## Hard rules

- **Never a hook the content does not pay off.** An overpromising headline is the same s41
  exposure as an overpromising price, and readers punish it immediately and permanently.
- **No manufactured fear.** The real objections are strong enough; inventing a threat to sell the
  fix is misrepresentation in a softer register.
- **The dignity line applies to every frame.** Never cleaning-as-a-woman's-duty, never "maid" or
  "the help", never a customer shamed for the state of their home. Most Xooters are women, and
  the ads tell them what the company thinks they are.
- **BRAND_GUIDE bans** `cheap`, `budget`, `discount`, `bargain` in any hook.
- **No price in a hook** unless the founder asks. Link the live page.
