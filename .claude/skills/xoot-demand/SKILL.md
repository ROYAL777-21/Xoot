# Xoot demand

## There are only four ways to tell anyone anything

1-to-1 warm · 1-to-many warm (content) · 1-to-1 cold · 1-to-many cold (paid).

If leads are not arriving, one of these four is not being done with enough skill or volume.
Everything else is a subcategory.

**Order of return at Xoot's stage:**

1. **Content to a warm-ish local audience** — the blog and social programme. Compounds, cheap,
   and the only channel that also earns AI citations.
2. **Xooter recruitment content** — relieves the supply constraint, which is the real ceiling.
3. **Warm outreach** — the founder's own network, for the first customers and the first reviews.
4. **Paid** — last, and only once the funnel converts. Paid traffic into a broken funnel buys a
   precise measurement of how broken it is, at retail price.

## ⚠️ Supply first

This is a marketplace. Demand that outruns Xooter coverage produces cancellations, refunds and
one-star reviews — marketing spend converted into reputational damage that costs more to repair
than the bookings were worth.

**Before any demand campaign: check coverage in the target suburbs.** If coverage is thin, the
correct campaign is recruitment.

## Warm outreach — the first customers

The founder has a list; it is in their phone and their existing accounts. The sequence:
personalise the opening from something actually known about the person, acknowledge what they
say, compliment it honestly, ask a further question, and let the offer arrive naturally.

**Make the first one easy to say yes to.** A free or heavily-supported first clean in exchange
for permission to use the review and photography is the cheapest possible acquisition of the
asset every later channel depends on. Ten to twenty of these before launch is worth more than
any amount of pre-launch content.

Then keep the list warm with periodic value, and a short direct probe: *are you still looking to
get your Saturdays back?*

## Content — give before asking

**At most one ask per five posts**, every channel. People are waiting for the ask; when it does
not come, trust accrues and sharing increases. When it finally comes, it converts.

Structure every unit as **hook → retain → reward**. Curiosity opens the loop, the payoff closes
it. Never open a loop the content does not close.

## Cold outreach

A numbers game with a trust deficit. Close the deficit by giving something real and specific
before asking for anything — for property managers and small offices, a genuinely useful
turnover checklist or a costed comparison beats any pitch.

Follow up more times, in more ways, than feels comfortable. Expect several touches before a
first booking. Respect POPIA: consent, an identifiable sender, and a working opt-out on every
electronic message.

## Getting booked leads to show up

Four levers, in order of impact:

| Lever | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Speed** | Respond within minutes. Book same-day or next-day, never more than three days out |
| **Availability** | More slots, more days, finer granularity. Self-service booking alongside assisted |
| **Personalisation** | Their preferred channel. Proof from someone like them, in their suburb |
| **Volume** | More reminders, across more channels, than feels necessary |

**Pull forward whenever possible** — an earlier slot converts better than a later one, because
intent decays.

## Harvesting proof — the marketing machine

The goal is a business whose ads come from its users, not its founder. A founder-dependent ad
account is also an unsellable one.

Sources, cheapest first, all of which Xoot will already be generating:

1. **Support and chat wins** — screenshot them (with consent).
2. **Reviews** — Google, app stores, in-app ratings.
3. **Social mentions** — a tag or hashtag, repurposed.
4. **Post-booking moments** — the "coming home to it done" reaction, asked for at the right time.
5. **Xooter stories** — earnings, independence, pride. These recruit *and* they sell trust to
   customers, because a company its workers speak well of is a company you let into your home.

**Written consent before any face, home or name appears.** A WhatsApp "sure" is not durable
consent under POPIA and it is her livelihood on a billboard.

## Paid — the efficiency rule

Target **LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1**, and aim to recover acquisition cost inside 30 days so growth
self-funds.

**Validate creative organically before paying to distribute it.** A format that cleared the
3-second retention gate (see `xoot-hooks`) is a candidate for spend; one that has not is a
hypothesis you are paying to test at the worst possible price.

## Constraints that apply to everything here

- Every claim traced to code, the live ladder, or `docs/product/locked-commercial-facts.md`.
- No price in creative unless the founder asks — link the live page.
- `BRAND_GUIDE` bans `cheap`, `budget`, `discount`, `bargain`; never `subscribe` for Care+.
- Say **Xooter**. Never "cleaner", "maid" or "the help".
- No astroturfing, no sockpuppet accounts, no automated comment farms. In a market this small,
  being caught is terminal for a brand whose entire proposition is trust.
