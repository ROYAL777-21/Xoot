# Xoot retention

## Why this ranks above acquisition

A business losing 15% of customers a month loses 83% of them in a year and must replace all of it
to stand still. Churn sets the ceiling on what acquisition can ever be worth — every rand of CAC
is priced against LTV. Retention work raises the budget for everything else in the company.

## Find the activation behaviour before designing anything

There is usually one early behaviour that separates customers who stay from those who do not.
For Xoot the testable candidates are:

- A **second booking within 30 days**
- Being matched with the **same Xooter twice**
- Saving a household profile (access notes, pets, preferences)
- Setting a recurring schedule
- Rating a completed booking

**Measure which one predicts retention. Do not assume.** The point of the exercise is that the
answer is often counter-intuitive, and building onboarding around a non-predictive behaviour is
expensive and invisible. Once identified, onboarding has exactly one job: get a new customer to
that behaviour quickly.

## The strongest lever this product has

**Same-Xooter continuity.** It is hard to leave a person and easy to cancel a service. Continuity
is what Care+ is really selling, and it is worth more than any discount.

Second strongest: **act when the pattern breaks, not when they cancel.** A household that booked
fortnightly and has not booked in three weeks is the moment. By cancellation it is mostly over.

Third: **exit interviews save roughly half** of people who ask to cancel — but only if a human
speaks to them before the cancellation completes.

## ⚠️ The Care+ honesty rules — these are not guidance

**Care+ breaks even at 2.54 bookings per month.** Below that, the member pays more than
pay-as-you-go. A fortnightly household — a large share of the base — **loses money on Care+**.

Three hard rules follow:

1. **No savings claim unless it is true for that specific user's actual booking history.**
   Not true "on average", not true "for most members". True for them. No history → no claim.
   A blanket "members save money" is false for a large share of readers and is a s41 exposure.

2. **The cancellation flow shows the real number** — what they actually spent as a member, and
   what the same bookings would have cost at standard rates. **This will sometimes say Care+ cost
   them money, and it must still say it.** They were losing money, they would have worked it out,
   and telling them first is the difference between a churned customer and a complaint to the
   National Consumer Commission. It also makes the positive number credible when it is positive.

3. **Never downsell frequency.** If a customer chose twice a week, no surface may offer once a
   week. Present same-or-greater cadence only. Reducing someone's plan to retain them converts a
   good customer into a worse one and reads as a company that would rather have less of you.

**Selling Care+ to a segment it costs money is churn you paid to create.** Recommend it to the
people it genuinely serves — high-frequency households, people who value continuity above price
— and say plainly who it is not for. That honesty is a retention feature.

## Community and continuity

- Connect members to Xooters, not just to the app.
- Recognise good Xooters publicly; it raises their retention, and supply is the ceiling.
- Annual and prepaid members churn markedly less — Care+ paid annually is a retention product
  before it is a pricing one. Respect CPA s14: max 24 months, 20 business days' notice.

## Reporting rules

- **Never a single blended churn number.** By cohort, by segment, by acquisition channel. The
  only useful question is which channel brings customers who stay.
- **Always state `n`.** On a small base most differences are noise, and saying so is the job.
- **Report the losses.** A retention log that only records saves teaches nothing.

## Xooter retention is retention too

A Xooter who stops accepting jobs is a supply loss that caps demand. Track application → approved
→ first job → fifth job, and treat drop-off between those steps as churn, because it is.
