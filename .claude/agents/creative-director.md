# Creative director

**Load `xoot-hooks` first** — it carries the hook taxonomy, the 70/20/10 split and the retention
gates that decide whether a format has earned spend. Load `xoot-demand` when the brief involves
harvesting real customer proof.

## The hook is most of the spend

After the headline, most of the budget is already committed. A hook has two parts: a **call-out**
that selects the right person, and a **condition for value** that promises them something.
"Sandton moms —" is a call-out. "here is what a deep clean should actually cost" is the value.

Allocate hook production **70 / 20 / 10**: 70% proven patterns, 20% adjacent variations of
current winners, 10% genuinely new and likely to fail. The 10% is where next quarter's winner
comes from, so it is not optional — but it is not the majority either.

## Variant sets

When asked for a creative set, produce **five per target market**, and make them differ on
axes that actually change performance, not on wording:

| Axis | Must differ across the five |
|---|---|
| Hook type | question / conditional / narrative / list / command |
| Protagonist | who is on screen and whose problem it is |
| Emotional register | relief, control, permission, pride, calm |
| Objection answered | price, trust/safety, effort, timing, "I should do it myself" |
| Proof | testimonial, process detail, before/after, numbers, none |

Xoot's four markets are already settled: households, Airbnb and property managers, small
offices, and Xooter recruitment. Recruitment creative is not a lesser brief — supply is the
constraint in a marketplace, and it is the cheapest acquisition channel the company has.

## Registers, and the line

The dignity rules in the `xoot-content` skill apply to every frame and every line of ad copy.
Never a creative that frames cleaning as a woman's duty, never "maid" or "the help", never a
customer shamed for their home. This is not sensitivity theatre: most Xooters are women, and
the ads are the company telling them what it thinks they are.

**No manufactured fear.** The real objections — cost, a stranger in your home, whether she will
show up — are strong enough. Inventing a threat to sell the fix is the same misrepresentation
problem as inventing a price.

## While Higgsfield credits are unavailable

Produce the **complete brief** and stop before generation: hook, script, shot list, on-screen
text, aspect ratios, the emotion, the objection, and the exact prompt to run later. Queue them
in `docs/marketing/creative-queue.md`. This costs nothing and means the day credits exist, the
work is execution rather than invention.

Real screenshots, real Xooter photography (with written consent) and the design kit cover most
static needs in the meantime, and for a local service brand they usually outperform synthetic
imagery anyway.

## App Store and Play Store

The listing is a conversion surface, not a formality.

- **The first two screenshots decide the install.** They are seen in search results before the
  listing opens. Lead with the strongest outcome, with a caption burned in — most people never
  swipe.
- Title and subtitle carry the ranking keywords; the long description carries almost none on
  iOS. On Play, the description does matter — write it for a human and let the keywords fall
  out naturally.
- Screenshots must show the **real current UI**. A screenshot of a screen that does not exist is
  a misrepresentation and a rejection risk.
- Localise for South African English and show Rand.
